## Proposed spoken script

We're back for day two of our journey to Phandalin. Hey, surviving even one day is never a guarantee. Before opening Morelord Journeys, let's move our party five hexes down the High Road. That's the distance we covered yesterday.

Now we'll reopen our saved journey. Today we're leaving the High Road for part of the trip, so we'll change danger to Untamed wilderness and navigation to Routine. There are trails to follow, but they're a little less obvious than an entire road.

We'll resolve weather and pace, then jump ahead to the day encounter checks. Each player rolls in their own session, and the game master sees the results.

[If an encounter occurs:] We've got an encounter. Let's see how Morelord Encounters can help us prepare it.

[Otherwise:] No encounter this time. But let's see what we could have prepared if one had come up.

Take note of the player's perception score, as this will compete against the encounter's stealth score.

Let's open Encounters.  We'll choose Random Encounter. The game master controls the difficulty, so we can choose the level of challenge we want before generating anything. We'll verify our four party members and review the encounter sources. We can include or exclude books; for this example, we'll uncheck the Phandelver and Below bestiary.

Now let's generate our options. We have Pack Skirmish, Boss Battle, Boss and Minions, The Horde, an Elite or Mirror Team, and a Random mix. Different ways to make our party question their travel arrangements.

We'll use Boss and Minions. We want creatures that make sense on land, and actual monsters for this fight. We can replace just the boss or just the minions without throwing away the whole encounter. Let's try a couple of alternatives until the group fits.

That looks more appropriate. We'll select this encounter, then open the encounter map before placing the tokens.

We'll put our enemies near the buried sword statue and bring the party in toward the bottom of the map.

And now, for my favorite phrase in the game: Roll for Initiative! You let that play out.  Hopefully everyone survives, but rolling new characters is always fun too!

Back on the regional map, we'll reopen Journeys. For this example, we'll account for a third of a day spent on the encounter and a short rest. That time matters to our travel progress.

We'll resolve the intervening steps and jump ahead to Press On. Since we lost some time, the party wants to make up another third of a day. But pushing harder comes with a risk: everyone makes a Constitution saving throw, and a failed save can add exhaustion automatically.

Let's see each player's roll.

[If any fail:] We've picked up some exhaustion. That gives us something to think about when we make camp.

[If all pass:] Everyone made it through without gaining exhaustion. The risk was real; the dice were just feeling generous.

We'll finish the remaining travel steps and move ahead to camp. Normally, each character takes a watch. An exhausted character can sleep instead, but that watch needs someone else to cover it or it stays unguarded. Being tired does not make the monsters promise to behave.

[If any gained exhaustion:] We'll let our exhausted travelers sleep through their watch periods and review the gaps in our coverage.

[If none gained exhaustion:] We don't need to change the watches for new exhaustion tonight, but this is where we'd make that tradeoff.

Then we'll resolve the night and review everyone's rest. Recovery depends on the rest they actually get and their supplies; extra sleep alone doesn't guarantee the exhaustion disappears.

Finally, we'll check the expedition log and complete the travel day. That's day two: an encounter prepared, travel time accounted for, and a party learning that making up time can have a cost.
